# Monthly pick ownership reconstruction

The opening ledger reconstructs original ownership on December 31, 2021.
It has 119 reviewed allocation rules and a closed 420-pick reporting universe.
It is generated by `opening_baseline.py`; the filename retains `partial` only
for compatibility with the earlier retrieval work.

`pick_replay.py` applies dated source-reviewed actions from the 2022, 2023,
2024–25 and 2026 cohorts. Each source event must have a reviewed action. Missing
source events make the view incomplete; they never default to retained own picks.
Amendments assert their expected prior legal terms. Actual draft outcomes and
published deferral decisions enter only after their publication dates.

`pick_allocator.py` assigns each physical pick exactly once in every joint
outcome. Swaps change owners; they do not create additional picks. Ranked and
nested claims select actual held entitlements after earlier rights. Protected
rights that cannot convey do not require ownership of a pick owed under an older
claim. Conditional transfers still enforce their documented ownership guards.
The internal universe extends beyond the displayed seven-year window so that
protected rollovers can resolve without treating an undisplayed asset as zero.

Forfeiture allocation is chronological. Legal protection thresholds use actual
selection numbers after forfeiture compression, while the input joint outcomes
retain all 30 physical origins per round. First-round forfeitures affect the
lottery before allocation; they cannot be approximated by deleting a draw result.
The separate Monte Carlo module discloses assumptions for future NBA procedures
that have not yet been specified.

The reconstruction retains three explicit source interpretations:

- The February 2022 Derrick White trade's double-protected-first fallback was
  reported contemporaneously as a Dallas-or-Miami second. Boston's previously
  acquired economic entitlement can instead have Houston as its physical
  component under the older Harden allocation. The model transfers Boston's
  single held component of that reviewed pool, with this inference recorded.
- The July 2026 Collins consideration was reported as a heavily protected,
  most-favorable Detroit-held 2028 second. The retrospective source's raw
  Detroit label conflicts with an older Philadelphia claim. The reconstruction
  therefore uses the reported best retained Detroit entitlement and applies
  its top-55 protection, documenting that interpretation.
- In a rare counterfactual 2025 second-round ordering, the public Kuzma and
  Smart summaries imply overlapping components after older Boston, Utah and
  New York claims. Milwaukee receives its earlier second-best held claim;
  Memphis receives the least remaining Washington-held component. The modeled
  residual interpretation is recorded explicitly and preserves both ownership
  guards and physical conservation.

Josh Hart's February 2023 trade has a separate, temporary economic claim because
its four-second fallback did not publicly identify physical origins and years.
Until New York's April 2 playoff clinch, a protected-first failure produces an
explicit four-generic-second EV debit to New York and equal credit to Portland.
It does not invent physical picks. The quote distribution and uncertainty range
are exposed to the valuation layer.

Validation includes dated source-event completeness, physical conservation,
ownership guards over joint forecast paths, and targeted boundary tests. The
final historical run prices all 59 monthly dates with 10,000 joint outcomes per
date, includes all 30 teams, and reconciles every league total. Passing
these checks does not by itself validate the win forecast, pick production curve,
or assumptions about unannounced future lottery procedures.
